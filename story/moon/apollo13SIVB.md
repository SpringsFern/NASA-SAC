## crashing rockets for science

This one is a little different. It's not a lander, it's a crater. After the Saturn V third stage finished pushing Apollo 13 toward the Moon, NASA didn't just let it drift. They steered it straight into the surface on purpose.

Why? Because the Apollo 12 crew had left a seismometer behind, and a crash of known size at a known spot is a perfect way to calibrate it. The stage hit on 14 April 1970, about 135 km from that seismometer, and made a crater roughly 30 meters across. The vibrations helped scientists figure out how seismic waves move through the Moon's crust.

They did this with several rocket stages and empty ascent stages. The whole seismic network ended up recording over 13,000 events. So this random-looking crater is actually a leftover from a real experiment. You can spot it in LRO photos today.
