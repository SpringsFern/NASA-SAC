## the robot that listened to Mars breathe

InSight landed in 2018 and didn't move around at all. Instead it placed a super-sensitive seismometer on the ground and just listened. Over four years it picked up more than 1,300 marsquakes and used them to map out the inside of Mars, including its core. First time we'd really seen another planet's insides like that.

The problem was dust. It slowly piled up on the solar panels, and there was no wind to clean it off. Power kept dropping. The team took a final, very dusty selfie in April 2022 and put the robotic arm into its "retirement pose." The last signal came on 15 December 2022, and NASA called it on 21 December.

InSight is still sitting on Elysium Planitia, quiet now, but the data it sent back is going to keep scientists busy for decades.
