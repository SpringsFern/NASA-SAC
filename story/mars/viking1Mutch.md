## the first one to make it work

Viking 1 landed on 20 July 1976 and became the first U.S. spacecraft to survive and work on Mars. It sent back the first panoramas of the surface, dug up soil, and ran biology experiments that scientists are still arguing about today.

It kept going for about six years. The ending is kind of tragic and kind of dumb. In November 1982, ground control sent a command to update the battery-charging software. The command accidentally overwrote the data the antenna used to point at Earth. So the lander was probably fine, it just couldn't aim its antenna at us anymore. NASA tried to reach it for months and never heard back.

It's still out there on Chryse Planitia. Its twin, Viking 2, is over in Utopia Planitia, quiet since 1980. Two little 1970s robots, still parked on Mars.
